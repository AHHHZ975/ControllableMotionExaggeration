Beyond physical realism

Abstract

Recent motion generative models synthesize physically plausible character motion, but often overlook the animation principles that professionals use to make characters feel alive, expressive, and engaging. We focus on exaggeration and study how to incorporate it into modern text-to-motion pipelines.

We introduce a two-stage framework. At training time, we fine-tune pre-trained motion models on a curated, graded exaggeration dataset. At inference time, we formulate exaggeration with dynamic movement primitives (DMPs) and use that formulation to guide diffusion and flow-matching models—without additional training. Across MDM, KIMODO, and HY-Motion, our methods increase expressiveness while preserving the neutral motion's intent and physical plausibility.

How can a generative motion model amplify expression while keeping the action recognizable and the character believable?

Two complementary paths

Methodology

Training-time

Learn exaggeration as a native attribute

ExMoCap contains 26 actions, each performed at five intensity levels from neutral to extremely exaggerated. We retarget and augment these performances, then fine-tune motion generators with LoRA and prior preservation.

  1. Capture graded performances.
  2. Retarget to each model's native representation.
  3. Fine-tune with text-conditioned intensity labels.
Inference-time

Guide a frozen model with DMPs

Per-interval DMPs decompose motion between key poses. Scaling the forcing term amplifies the expressive trajectory shape while preserving interval boundaries. Guided sampling then pulls a frozen generator toward that target.

  1. Generate a neutral reference.
  2. Build a DMP-exaggerated target.
  3. Guide denoising while retaining the learned motion prior.

The central trade-off

SFT provides the strongest direct control over requested exaggeration levels. Training-free guidance recovers plausibility compared with unconstrained DMP amplification. SFT + TF yields the strongest physical exaggeration, with a larger departure from the neutral action.

Conclusion

Animation principles as generative controls

Our results show that exaggeration can be learned as a controllable attribute or injected through inference-time guidance. More broadly, they point toward motion foundation models that understand not only how people move, but how animators design movement for clarity, appeal, and expression.
